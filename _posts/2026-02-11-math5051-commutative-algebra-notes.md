These are my notes on commutative algebra for MATH5051. In the first semester of my fourth undergraduate year, I took MATH5051 Abstract Algebra I, taught by Prof. Jiu Kang Yu. The course focused mainly on commutative algebra and also served as preparation for CUHK's Ph.D. qualifying examination in algebra. (I felt that the material he covered went well beyond the level of the qualifying examination.)

The main threads were:

1. Various versions of Hilbert's Nullstellensatz and their consequences.
2. Dimension theory. We proved that, for a local Noetherian ring, the Krull, Chevalley, and Samuel dimensions are equal, and then used this theorem to study special classes of rings, such as normal and regular rings.
3. Serre's criterion for regular rings, proved using tools from homological algebra.

The course moved quickly and covered a great deal of material. Prof. Yu taught as though everyone in the class already had a very strong background, which I clearly did not. In these notes, I try to supply the details and explanations missing from his slides and lectures.
